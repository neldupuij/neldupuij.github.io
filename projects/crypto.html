Crypto market microstructure in kdb+/q

Where does the price of Bitcoin form, and what does it mean for a market maker? Three studies on Binance data: spot vs. perpetual lead-lag, flows around funding payments, and USDC → BTC routing costs.

q / kdb+PythonBinance aggTradesTardis order booksNov 2026

In short

  • Lead-lag. Over 1–8 November 2026, perpetual BTCUSDT trade prices move before spot ones on every one of the 8 days (lead-lag ratio 2.5–5.1 at 100 ms). Observed on trade prices; not a demonstrated exploitable opportunity at a few milliseconds.
  • Funding. The exploratory October effect did not replicate under a frozen protocol: 0 replications out of 5, no contradiction, differences of +0.01 to +0.25 bp with every 95% interval containing 0.
  • Routing. Without fees, the indirect route USDC → USDT → BTC is cheaper by under 1 bp at the median (1 and 5 BTC). At 1 bp per trade the median turns negative; at 10 bp no gain is observed that day.

01Spot vs. perpetual: who moves first?

Question
Between Binance BTCUSDT spot and USD-M perpetual trade prices, which one changes first?
Data
Binance aggregated trades, 1–8 November 2026, loaded into a date-partitioned kdb+ HDB.
Method
Prices on a time grid (last trade at or before each step, via as-of join); cross-correlation of returns at 100 ms and 10 ms; lead-lag ratio = Σcorr² for perp-leads lags ÷ Σcorr² for spot-leads lags; event study around large jumps (> 3 bp in 10 ms, at least 1 s apart).
Cross-correlation of returns, 10 ms grid8 Nov 2026 · hover a bar

corr(perp return at t, spot return at t + lag). Positive lags = perp moves first. The contemporaneous bar (lag 0) is the largest; the right side dominates the left.

Lead-lag ratio, day by dayratio > 1 ⇒ perp leads

Each of the 8 days, at both resolutions, the ratio is above 1: 2.5–5.1 at 100 ms, 2.7–7.3 at 10 ms. Most co-movement is still contemporaneous at 100 ms (correlation 0.63–0.70).

Event study: average path after a perpetual jump8 days · 567 perp jumps

Signed move in bp relative to the price just before the jump, averaged over events. The spot catches up mostly within the first tens of milliseconds.

Limits. Last-trade prices: the market that trades less often looks mechanically "late", which can inflate the ratio. Spot and perpetual run on separate matching engines and millisecond timestamp alignment was not verified. No latency, fees or executable prices: this is not a demonstrated trading opportunity. Eight consecutive days, one instrument, one venue.

02Flows around funding payments

Question
Do 8-hourly funding payments come with flows consistent with position unwinding, and with a measurable price move?
Method
Exploration on BTCUSDT, October 2026, then a frozen replication protocol (SHA fingerprint kept) applied unchanged to 5 new samples. ±60 min windows vs. placebo hours, day-level bootstrap.

03USDC → BTC: direct or via USDT?

Question
For the same net amount of BTC, is USDC → USDT → BTC cheaper than USDC → BTC?
Method
Tardis 25-level order books of the three pairs on 1 November 2026 (MD5-checked). One decision per second (86,399), as-of join on arrival time, market orders walked through visible levels, fees taken in the received currency, Binance tick sizes and minimums. No 1 USDC = 1 USDT assumption (the rate moved between 1.00094 and 1.00126). Rules written before any computation.

Median saving in bp (positive = indirect route cheaper) and, in brackets, the share of seconds where the indirect route is cheaper, among seconds where both routes can be evaluated. Fee levels are scenarios (historical tiers not verified). All savings are estimated on observed books, not executable: the book can move before the order arrives, and the two legs of the indirect route are not atomic.
